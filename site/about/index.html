About Anime Card Farm Wiki

Anime Card Farm Wiki is an independent player guide for the Roblox experience searched as Anime Card Farm / Anime Card Factory (place ID 125039473548047). We are not affiliated with Roblox Corporation or the experience creators. We do not claim to be an official wiki, partner, or support desk.

Why this site exists

Search results for this game’s codes and early progression are often undated mirrors: identical Active counts, missing Contested labels, and no next step after redeem. Players lose time pasting expired strings and buying packs before confirming income. We publish a dated Active/Contested table, a first-session route, and pack-versus-upgrade payback logic that uses your Shop numbers — not a copied “best pack” slogan.

What we publish

  • Codes — working-status table with sources, Contested honesty, failure checklist, and spelling traps.
  • Beginner guide — codes → weak-slot replace → payback → offline readiness.
  • Packs — payback formula with explicit example warnings when odds are unpublished.
  • Supporting hubs: cards, mutations, offline money, crafting (with honest unconfirmed limits), FAQ, official links.
  • Trust set: about, contact, privacy, terms, ads disclosure, editorial policy, source methodology.

What we refuse to invent

  • Active codes that only appear on undated TikTok or Discord mirrors.
  • Guaranteed pack odds or pity schedules without an official public table.
  • Fake in-game redeem screenshots or forged “Verified Oct 15” stamps.
  • Entity names, company registrations, or “official partner” badges we do not have.
  • Card DPS, mutation multipliers, or offline rates presented as studio data when we only have UI labels.

How we measure quality

We publish answer-first money pages, dated sources, Contested honesty, and a complete trust set. Codes, the first-hour route, and pack math each have their own hub.

What “wiki” means here

We use “wiki” in the fan-guide sense: structured reference pages with transparent editing rules. It does not mean anyone can anonymously overwrite Active labels without review. Corrections go through the contact email so Contested evidence is not destroyed by drive-by edits.

Correction process

  1. Email the URL, the wrong sentence, and what you checked (client UI, Roblox listing date, or a dated public list).
  2. We re-check the primary surface before flipping Active ↔ Expired.
  3. If sources disagree, the row stays Contested with both sides named — we do not silently pick the louder TikTok.

What we are not

  • Not Roblox Support, not the experience group, not a trading middleman.
  • Not a place to buy Robux, accounts, or “guaranteed legendaries.”
  • Not a mirror that republishes every rumor string as Active.

Sister-site honesty

If you arrived from another Roblox guide in the same operator set, expect the same rules: no fabricated codes, no AdSense UI tricks during getting-ready, and no claim of official partnership. English is canonical on this hostname.

Reader jobs we optimize for

Three jobs show up in search and in our contact mail: (1) “which codes work today,” (2) “what do I do in the first hour,” and (3) “should I buy this pack.” Each job gets a dedicated hub instead of one mega-page that ranks for nothing. Codes stays ruthless about dates. Beginner stays ruthless about sequence. Packs stays ruthless about math you can repeat with Shop numbers from your client.

Independence boundaries

Independence means we can mark a popular influencer code Contested when redeem fails. It also means we will not pretend a soft launch shout is a permanent Active list. When creators publish an official channel, we link it from official links and prefer it over aggregators — but we still re-check redeems on our stamp date.

Performance and crawl

Guides are plain HTML so a blocked script cannot hide a code or a redeem step. Depth lives on the hubs linked above.

Accessibility and clarity

Redeem steps and Active tables stay in plain HTML so blocked scripts cannot hide the answer. We avoid “download now” patterns that mimic malware landing pages. Tables include text labels, not color alone, for Active/Contested/Expired.

Maintenance promise

We would rather show Contested than invent Active. We would rather keep a short crafting page than publish a confident wrong recipe grid. That tradeoff is deliberate and will not be reversed for thin traffic spikes.
